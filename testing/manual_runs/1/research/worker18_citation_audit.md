## Overall disposition

The methods in `research/sources.md` generally have a direct, usable URL: Townsend, Beckman–O'Neil, DLMF, FFTLog, QDHT, Malinka, CGFHT, butterfly, FINUFFT, and Thompson all have DOI, arXiv, publisher, documentation, or repository links. The source table has two actionable gaps:

1. FMM is discussed in the worker reports but has no row or direct citation in `sources.md`. Add a Hankel/FMM reference (Kapur–Rokhlin) and, if the historical algorithm is mentioned, the original Greengard–Rokhlin paper.
2. Add the published DOI links for the butterfly papers alongside the arXiv links when those papers are used in the final report.

`README.md` and `research/log.md` do not themselves provide a literature claim that needs a citation, but the final report must keep published claims separate from repository measurements. The current implementation is a project-specific approximate blocked asymptotic/FFT plus direct-correction method; it is not an implementation of Townsend, Beckman–O'Neil, FFTLog, QDHT, CGFHT, a butterfly factorization, or FMM.

## Cross-file wording corrections for the final report

- Replace “Both are new exact-grid specializations” with “This project proposes/adapts two exact-grid specializations; no publication claim is made.” A literature search cannot support a universal novelty claim without a systematic search and a carefully delimited prior-art statement.
- Reserve “exact” for the target definition, index/phase algebra, or an explicitly proved identity. For floating-point execution use “approximate,” “grid-aligned DFT,” or “no interpolation in this block.” In particular, an ordinary FFT is not an exact binary64 evaluation of the Bessel matrix.
- Replace “Neumann correction collapses” with “the chosen zero-perturbation representation does not require a Neumann correction; the current implementation does not use that theorem/step.”
- Replace universal negatives such as “no exact identity,” “no method can,” or “FMM fails” with “no such identity was found/proved here,” “was not established by the cited source,” or “was not competitive in the tested local experiments.”
- Keep published theorems and project measurements separate. The current `src/dht_asym.c` uses ratio-4 geometric bands, a direct near-field Bessel table, full `N`-point FFTs per asymptotic order/band, partner-bin reconstruction for complex input, and Kahan direct sums. It contains no generic NUFHT, QDHT, FFTLog, CGFHT, butterfly, or FMM implementation.
- Do not use the repository’s screening numbers as a full acceptance certificate. `research/log.md` records a residual-sign failure for the `(K=12,z0=18,r=4)` candidate and does not certify the current default `(K=11,z0=23,r=4)` for arbitrary complex `N=65536` input at the stated gate. The source comment claiming a “fastest profile retained” is not supported by the cited literature or a complete archive-level benchmark.
- When describing the current complexity, do not quote Townsend’s sharper `O(N(log N)^2/log log N)` result as the implementation’s complexity. State the project’s own full-FFT accounting, with its dependence on asymptotic order, band count, and direct-entry count.
- For FFTLog, QDHT, and CGFHT, say that their native grids/integrals differ from the target and that any resampling or reconstruction route is an adapter requiring its own error and input-dependent work. “Does not preserve the target matrix” is appropriate only when qualified as “without a separately justified adapter”; it is not a claim that no adapter can ever exist.
- For butterfly and FMM, distinguish the published structural/algorithmic framework from the project’s sampled rank experiments and non-implementation. Local rank evidence can motivate a design decision, but cannot certify global complementary low rank, impossibility, or exactness.
