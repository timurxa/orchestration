# S2-06 — concurrency/executor adversarial audit

**Scope:** current `implementation_spec.md` §§4.2–4.3, 5.1/P15; `key_correctness_theorems.md` P15 and proof sketch; S2-06 DAG acceptance. Read-only. No F* or tests. All findings assess contract targets, not proved theorems.

## Verdict

The prose names sound safety principles: only distinct ready work may overlap; claims bind occurrence, identity, exact input and allocation; outputs stay invisible until authorized append; appends serialize and stale heads require reload/reconstruction/reauthorization; crashes discard volatile state; committed local/generator work is reused. Sparse `ReadyInputAgreement` correctly identifies why unrelated earlier nodes need not block execution.

S2-06 still lacks an explicit abstract controller state and step relation. P15 says “every permitted schedule,” but permitted steps, atomic claim ownership, append/rejection races, and crash cuts are not formalized. Existing acceptance is therefore a target, not established by a checkable interleaving theorem. No source evidence discharges P15; S2-00 says controller, claims, and interleaving relation are absent.

## Specific gaps

1. **No operational semantics.** Define abstract `ControllerState` (live claims, task results, adapter keys, append slot, observed head) and atomic steps: claim, local/generator return, intent dispatch, adapter return, authorize, append commit/reject, crash/restart. State which values are volatile and which head is durable. Define scheduler bound and one-controller/multi-controller scope.
2. **Claim establishment.** Prove invariant from transition relation, not from “claims are distinct” as scheduler precondition alone. A claim must be fresh for pending replay occurrence and hold `(occurrence, exact pinned identity, reconstructed input, allocation, base head)`. Result carries that claim token; cannot swap occurrence/input/allocation or update head to rescue it. Clarify whether head is merely freshness token, since unrelated appends can leave all bound values valid but still require explicit reauthorization.
3. **Reauthorization/frame theorem.** For every other legal append `e_q`, `q != p`, show either p's readiness/input/allocation/identity are unchanged, or p is rederived from new state. If `e_q` commits a referenced predecessor, p was not ready before it; after commit p may newly become ready and must be claimed from new state. If state changed so p is no longer pending (possible duplicate/multi-controller race), discard result. Do not reuse an authorization witness from old head.
4. **Ready-input quantifiers.** State `ReadyInputAgreement(S,p,x)` for every ready occurrence and every full legal environment agreeing with sparse references. Show constants/root input included; generated child root wire and dependent indices use exact committed generator input; external outputs range over every declared inhabitant. This lemma allows concurrent evaluation without waiting for unrelated topological prefix.
5. **Serial append and publication.** Every event append is one current-head authorized action; only one append in flight per coordinator; stale/invalid rejection is a no-op. Define commit point as visibility point. Results/artifact refs remain task-local or unreferenced until commit; concurrent workers cannot read another worker's uncommitted result. Artifact backing claims require P9.
6. **Crash theorem domain.** Quantify every finite prefix/schedule and every process-exit cut, including cut during compute, authorization, append, and after commit/before acknowledgement. `restart` reloads exact latest prefix (P10); volatile claim/result sets empty; derive new enabled actions from replay. P15 handles abstract safety, P10 storage refinement, P13 concrete process/controller refinement; keep these boundaries explicit.
7. **Theorem statement.** Give an inductive invariant over `(J, controller_state)` and all step labels, then prove P15 for every finite permitted execution prefix and every crash/restart continuation. Include: unique live claims; claim/result binding; readiness; typed exact inputs; allocation ownership; append authorization; committed-only visibility; event provenance; no committed local/gen redispatch; pending-call same-key resume; replay/WF and ledger preservation. Define whether P15 concludes only safety or also reachability of every permitted event; fairness belongs to P11/P14.

## Strongest objection and residual

**Strongest objection:** uniqueness and staleness are currently prose constraints on an unnamed abstract controller, not consequences of a defined transition system. For two simultaneous claim attempts, no formal atomicity rule identifies which claim wins; for a stale result, “discard or reauthorize” does not specify a proof-preserving reauthorization transition. Thus P15's all-schedule claim has no precise schedule relation over which it can be proved yet.

**Residual boundary:** multi-controller execution is left conditional (“if multiple controllers ... shared fencing”). S2-06 must choose single abstract controller, or include globally fenced claim ownership; serial journal writes alone prevent conflicting durable commits but do not establish the stated global unique-live-claim property. If unique claim is per-controller, say so and prove cross-controller duplicate work cannot violate safety (pure local/generator retries may duplicate precommit, but only one append can win).

**Evidence status:** S2-06/P15/P10/P13 are contract/theorem targets here. No F* proof, test, or concrete controller behavior was inspected or claimed.
